🎓 **Ph.D. Completed at Johns Hopkins University**

I have completed my **Ph.D. in Materials Science and Engineering at Johns Hopkins University** under the guidance of **Prof. Timothy P. Weihs**.

My dissertation was titled:

**“Tailoring Magnesium Alloys for Biodegradable Implants From Atoms to Applications.”**

---

### Dissertation & Research

My doctoral research focused on understanding how **materials processing, microstructure, defects, corrosion, and mechanical behavior** interact in biodegradable magnesium alloys.

Over the course of the Ph.D., this expanded into several interconnected research directions, including:

- thermomechanical processing and microstructure control in lean magnesium alloys
- biodegradable metallic implants and corrosion
- vacancy and defect engineering
- advanced materials characterization
- machine-learning-guided materials design
- processing-structure-property relationships across multiple length scales

A central theme throughout the work was understanding materials fundamentally, then using that understanding to determine how processing can make them perform better.

---

### Collaborations & Mentorship

My doctoral work involved collaborations across **Johns Hopkins University, Pacific Northwest National Laboratory, the National Institute of Standards and Technology**, and several external research groups.

I was also fortunate to mentor master's, undergraduate, and high school researchers whose work contributed to publications, fellowships, research awards, and subsequent graduate study.
