🧠 **Comment Article Published in *Nature Reviews Materials***

Excited to share our new Comment, **“A call to elevate the role of processing in AI-driven materials design,”** co-authored by **Sreenivas Raguraman**, **Adam Griebel**, **Maitreyee Sharma Priyadharshini**, **Paulette Clancy**, and **Timothy P. Weihs**. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

### What the Piece Argues  
AI is accelerating materials discovery — but **processing** still decides whether predicted materials can actually be made and perform as intended. We advocate making processing a **first-class design axis** in models, datasets, and workflows, not an afterthought. (See p.1 for the core thesis.) :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

### Key Ideas & Examples  
- **Processing drives properties.** Grain size, texture, phase morphology, defect populations, and interface chemistry set strength, corrosion, transport, and biocompatibility (p.1). :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}  
- **Case studies in Mg alloys.** For ZX10, ECAP + annealing cut corrosion ~4× while retaining high yield strength via grain refinement and controlled precipitates; in Mg–9Al, microstructure governed *in vitro* vs *in vivo* Al release through speciation and retention (p.1). :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}  
- **What ML needs.** Processing-aware datasets with complete histories (order, timing, conditions) and standardized microstructural descriptors, plus methods like Bayesian optimization and uncertainty-aware modeling to learn from **small but high-quality data** (p.2). :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

---

### The Framework  
The **closed-loop, AI-integrated workflow** places *composition and processing* on equal footing: train models with processing features → recommend composition–processing candidates → synthesize, process, and characterize → update the dataset (see **Fig. 1** on p.2). :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

> “The future of materials design lies not just in what we might make, but also in **how** we make it.” :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}
