I am an <strong>experimental materials scientist</strong> interested in designing metallic materials from the atomic scale to application. My research focuses on how <strong>defects, chemistry, microstructure, and non-equilibrium processing</strong> interact to control material behavior, and how these relationships can be engineered to achieve targeted performance.

I combine <strong>advanced characterization, materials processing, and data-driven approaches</strong> to understand and design metallic materials for demanding environments. My research interests span <strong>defect and vacancy engineering</strong>, <strong>irradiation and radiation-tolerant materials</strong>, <strong>metallic biomaterials</strong>, and <strong>data-driven materials processing</strong>. Across these areas, I am particularly interested in connecting atomic-scale mechanisms and measurable defect states with processing, properties, and the practical requirements that determine whether a material ultimately succeeds in an application.

I am currently a <strong>Postdoctoral Researcher</strong> in the <a href="https://metphys.mat.ethz.ch/" target="_blank"><strong>Laboratory of Metal Physics and Technology (LMPT) at ETH Zürich</strong></a>, working with <a href="https://mat.ethz.ch/department/people/person-detail.loeffler.html" target="_blank"><strong>Prof. Jörg F. Löffler</strong></a>. My current research explores <strong>defect engineering in crystalline and amorphous metals</strong>, including the characterization and manipulation of vacancies and free volume, their evolution under irradiation and non-equilibrium processing, and their influence on material behavior.

I completed my <strong>Ph.D. in Materials Science and Engineering at Johns Hopkins University</strong> with <a href="https://engineering.jhu.edu/faculty/timothy-weihs/" target="_blank"><strong>Prof. Timothy P. Weihs</strong></a>. My doctoral research centered on <strong>processing-structure-property relationships in metallic biomaterials</strong>, particularly biodegradable magnesium alloys, while also developing data-driven approaches for materials discovery and process optimization. During my Ph.D., I was a <strong>Visiting Researcher at Pacific Northwest National Laboratory</strong> and a <strong>Foreign Guest Researcher at the National Institute of Standards and Technology</strong>.

I earned my <strong>B.Tech. in Metallurgical and Materials Engineering from the National Institute of Technology Tiruchirappalli</strong>. During my undergraduate studies, I spent two summers as a research intern at the <strong>Indian Institute of Science</strong> and the <strong>National University of Singapore</strong>.
